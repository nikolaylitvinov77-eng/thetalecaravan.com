Why the Sun and the Moon Live in the Sky

Nigeria · Episode 10 · Nigerian folk tale

Tonight, the caravan stops in Nigeria.

Long ago the Sun and the Water were great friends on the earth. The Water will not visit: its fish and crabs would leave no room. So the Sun and the Moon build a huge house for their friend.

About the tale

Elphinstone Dayrell, District Commissioner in Southern Nigeria, published this tale in London in 1910, in his book “Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria, West Africa”. He does not say who told it to him; other tales in the same book mention Calabar and the Efik and Ibibio peoples. Andrew Lang, who wrote the introduction, noted that this explanation of why the Sun and the Moon left the earth was, as far as he knew, “without parallel elsewhere”. In 1968 Houghton Mifflin published the tale as a picture book illustrated by Blair Lent, and in 1969 the book received a Caldecott Honor for Lent’s pictures. The grandmother who tells the tale and the Sun’s evening visits to the Water are our own.

A Nigerian folk tale, retold from “Why the Sun and the Moon live in the Sky” in Elphinstone Dayrell’s “Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria, West Africa” (1910).
